# The Major Scale

## Description

The major scale is a fundamental building block in Western music and is one of the most common scales used in popular music. It's a diatonic scale, which means it has seven notes. Understanding the major scale is crucial for learning about melody, harmony, and song construction.

## Content

The major scale is built using a specific pattern of whole and half steps. A "whole step" is a distance of two frets on the guitar, and a "half step" is a distance of one fret.

The formula for the major scale is:

**Whole - Whole - Half - Whole - Whole - Whole - Half**

Starting from any root note, you can use this formula to find all the notes in that major scale.

For example, let's build a C Major scale, which has no sharps or flats:

- **C** (Root)
- (Whole step) -> **D**
- (Whole step) -> **E**
- (Half step) -> **F**
- (Whole step) -> **G**
- (Whole step) -> **A**
- (Whole step) -> **B**
- (Half step) -> **C** (Octave)

So, the notes in the C Major scale are: C, D, E, F, G, A, B.

On the guitar, the major scale can be played in many different positions on the fretboard. One common method for learning these positions is the "CAGED" system, which is based on the five open chord shapes: C, A, G, E, and D.
