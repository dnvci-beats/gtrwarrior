# Modes of the Major Scale

## Description

Modes are scales that are derived from a "parent" scale. The modes of the major scale are seven different scales that are created by starting the major scale on a different note. Each mode has a unique character and sound.

## Content

Let's use the C Major scale as our parent scale. The notes are C, D, E, F, G, A, B.

### The 7 Modes

1.  **Ionian (The Major Scale):**
    -   Starts on the 1st degree (C).
    -   Formula: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
    -   Sound: Happy, bright. The standard major sound.
    -   C Ionian: C D E F G A B

2.  **Dorian:**
    -   Starts on the 2nd degree (D).
    -   Formula: 1 2 b3 4 5 6 b7
    -   Sound: Minor, but with a "jazzy" or "funky" feel due to the major 6th.
    -   D Dorian: D E F G A B C

3.  **Phrygian:**
    -   Starts on the 3rd degree (E).
    -   Formula: 1 b2 b3 4 5 b6 b7
    -   Sound: Minor, with a dark, "Spanish" or "flamenco" sound due to the flat 2nd.
    -   E Phrygian: E F G A B C D

4.  **Lydian:**
    -   Starts on the 4th degree (F).
    -   Formula: 1 2 3 #4 5 6 7
    -   Sound: Major, but with a dreamy, "magical" or "ethereal" sound due to the sharp 4th.
    -   F Lydian: F G A B C D E

5.  **Mixolydian:**
    -   Starts on the 5th degree (G).
    -   Formula: 1 2 3 4 5 6 b7
    -   Sound: Major, but with a "bluesy" or "rock" sound due to the flat 7th. This is the scale that naturally fits over a dominant 7th chord.
    -   G Mixolydian: G A B C D E F

6.  **Aeolian (The Natural Minor Scale):**
    -   Starts on the 6th degree (A).
    -   Formula: 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7
    -   Sound: The standard minor sound. Sad, melancholic.
    -   A Aeolian: A B C D E F G

7.  **Locrian:**
    -   Starts on the 7th degree (B).
    -   Formula: 1 b2 b3 4 b5 b6 b7
    -   Sound: Dissonant and unstable due to the flat 5th. It is the least common mode in popular music.
    -   B Locrian: B C D E F G A

### How to Use Modes

Modes allow you to add different flavors to your playing. Instead of just playing a major or minor scale over a chord progression, you can use a mode that matches the underlying chord to create a more sophisticated sound. For example, over a Dm7 chord in the key of C, you can play D Dorian instead of just C major.
