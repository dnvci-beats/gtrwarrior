# Basic Chord Construction: Triads

## Description

A triad is a chord made up of three notes. Triads are the fundamental building blocks of harmony in most Western music. Understanding how to build triads is an essential first step in learning about chords and progressions.

## Content

Triads are built by stacking two intervals of a third on top of a root note. The specific type of thirds (major or minor) determines the quality of the triad (major, minor, diminished, or augmented).

The three most common types of triads are:
- Major Triad
- Minor Triad
- Diminished Triad

These triads are built using notes from the major scale.

### Major Triad

A major triad is built from the **1st**, **3rd**, and **5th** degrees of the major scale. It has a happy, bright sound.

- **Formula:** 1 - 3 - 5
- **Example (C Major):**
  - The C Major scale is C, D, E, F, G, A, B.
  - The 1st note is C.
  - The 3rd note is E.
  - The 5th note is G.
  - The C Major triad is **C - E - G**.

### Minor Triad

A minor triad is built from the **1st**, **flatted 3rd (b3)**, and **5th** degrees of the major scale. It has a sad, dark sound.

- **Formula:** 1 - b3 - 5
- **Example (C Minor):**
  - Start with the C Major triad (C - E - G).
  - Flatten the 3rd note (E becomes Eb).
  - The C Minor triad is **C - Eb - G**.

### Diminished Triad

A diminished triad is built from the **1st**, **flatted 3rd (b3)**, and **flatted 5th (b5)** degrees of the major scale. It has a tense, unstable sound.

- **Formula:** 1 - b3 - b5
- **Example (C Diminished):**
  - Start with the C Minor triad (C - Eb - G).
  - Flatten the 5th note (G becomes Gb).
  - The C Diminished triad is **C - Eb - Gb**.
