# Reading Sheet Music for Guitar

## Description

Reading standard music notation (sheet music) is a valuable skill for any musician, including guitarists. While many guitarists rely on tablature (tabs), sheet music provides a more complete picture of the music, including both pitch and rhythm. This guide covers the absolute basics.

## Content

### The Staff

Sheet music is written on a **staff** (or stave), which consists of five horizontal lines and four spaces. Each line and space represents a different musical pitch.

### The Treble Clef

Guitar music is written in the **treble clef**, which is the large, ornate symbol at the beginning of the staff. The treble clef curls around the second line from the bottom, which indicates that this line is the note G. This is why it's also called the "G clef".

### Notes on the Staff

The notes are placed on the lines and in the spaces. You can remember the notes on the lines with the mnemonic "Every Good Boy Does Fine" (E, G, B, D, F) from bottom to top. The notes in the spaces spell out "FACE" from bottom to top.

-   **Lines (bottom to top):** E G B D F
-   **Spaces (bottom to top):** F A C E

Notes can also be placed above or below the staff using **ledger lines**.

### Note Durations (Rhythm)

The shape of a note tells you how long it should be held.

-   **Whole Note:** An open oval. Lasts for four beats in 4/4 time.
-   **Half Note:** An oval with a stem. Lasts for two beats.
-   **Quarter Note:** A filled-in oval with a stem. Lasts for one beat.
-   **Eighth Note:** A quarter note with a flag. Lasts for half a beat. Two eighth notes are often connected with a beam.
-   **Sixteenth Note:** A quarter note with two flags. Lasts for a quarter of a beat.

### The Guitar's Range and Transposition

The guitar is a **transposing instrument**. It sounds one octave lower than it is written in sheet music. This is done to avoid using excessive ledger lines and make the music easier to read. The notes on the staff correspond to notes on the guitar, but they will sound an octave lower. For example, the middle C on a piano is written in the first ledger line space below the treble clef staff. On a guitar, that same written note is played on the 3rd fret of the A string, but it sounds an octave lower than the piano's middle C.

### Key Signatures and Time Signatures

-   **Time Signature:** Appears at the beginning of the music, telling you the meter (e.g., 4/4, 3/4).
-   **Key Signature:** A set of sharps (#) or flats (b) placed after the clef, telling you which notes to play sharp or flat throughout the piece.
