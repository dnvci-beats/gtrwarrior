# Cadences

## Description

In music, a cadence is a melodic or harmonic configuration that creates a sense of resolution or pause. Cadences are like punctuation in music; they mark the end of phrases, sections, or entire pieces. Understanding cadences is key to understanding the structure of music.

## Content

Cadences are typically defined by the last two chords of a musical phrase. There are four main types of cadences:

### 1. Authentic Cadence (V - I)

-   **Chords:** The V chord resolving to the I chord (e.g., G to C in the key of C).
-   **Sound:** This is the strongest and most final-sounding cadence. It provides a full sense of closure and is often found at the very end of a piece of music.
-   **Types:**
    -   **Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC):** The strongest type. Both chords are in root position, and the tonic note is in the highest voice of the I chord.
    -   **Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC):** A slightly weaker version where one of the chords is inverted or the tonic is not in the highest voice.

### 2. Plagal Cadence (IV - I)

-   **Chords:** The IV chord resolving to the I chord (e.g., F to C in the key of C).
-   **Sound:** Also sounds final, but less strong and conclusive than an authentic cadence. It has a "gentler" or more "solemn" feel. It's often called the "Amen" cadence because it's famously used to set the word "Amen" at the end of hymns.

### 3. Half Cadence (? - V)

-   **Chords:** Any chord resolving to the V chord (e.g., I-V, ii-V, IV-V).
-   **Sound:** This cadence sounds unfinished or like a pause. It creates an expectation that the music will continue, as the V chord wants to resolve. It's like a comma in a sentence.

### 4. Deceptive Cadence (V - vi)

-   **Chords:** The V chord resolving to the vi chord (e.g., G to Am in the key of C).
-   **Sound:** This cadence is called "deceptive" because it sets up the listener to expect a resolution to the tonic (I), but instead moves to the relative minor (vi). It's a surprise that creates interest and can be used to extend a musical phrase.

| Cadence   | Chord Progression | Sound         |
| --------- | ----------------- | ------------- |

| Plagal    | IV - I            | Final, gentle |
