# The Minor Pentatonic Scale

## Description

The minor pentatonic scale is one of the most widely used scales in rock, blues, and pop music. It's a five-note scale (penta-tonic) that is relatively easy to learn and apply, making it a favorite for improvisation and writing riffs and solos.

## Content

The minor pentatonic scale is derived from the natural minor scale by removing two notes: the 2nd and the 6th degrees.

The formula for the natural minor scale is:
**Whole - Half - Whole - Whole - Half - Whole - Whole**

Let's take the A natural minor scale as an example:
A (1), B (2), C (3), D (4), E (5), F (6), G (7)

To get the A minor pentatonic scale, we remove the 2nd note (B) and the 6th note (F). This leaves us with:

**A, C, D, E, G**

The interval formula for the minor pentatonic scale is:
**Minor Third - Whole Step - Whole Step - Minor Third - Whole Step**

(A to C is a minor third, C to D is a whole step, etc.)

This scale is very popular on the guitar because the patterns are easy to remember and play. The most common shape for the minor pentatonic scale is often referred to as "box 1" and is a staple for many guitarists.
