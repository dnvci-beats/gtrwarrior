# Acoustic Guitar Body Shapes

## Description

Acoustic guitars come in a wide variety of body shapes and sizes. The shape and size of the guitar's body have a significant impact on its tone, volume, and playing comfort. Understanding the common body shapes can help you choose the right guitar for your style and needs.

## Content

Here are some of the most common acoustic guitar body shapes, from smallest to largest.

### Parlor

-   **Description:** A very small and compact body shape, popular in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
-   **Sound:** Quiet and focused, with a strong midrange. It's not a loud, boomy guitar.
-   **Best for:** Fingerstyle playing, blues, and players who want a comfortable, intimate-sounding guitar.

### Concert (0) and Grand Concert (00)

-   **Description:** These are still small-bodied guitars, but slightly larger than a parlor. The "0" refers to a sizing system from Martin Guitars.
-   **Sound:** Balanced and articulate. They have a clear high end and a controlled low end, making them great for recording.
-   **Best for:** Fingerstyle players and those who find larger guitars uncomfortable.

### Auditorium / Grand Auditorium (000 / GA)

-   **Description:** This is a very popular mid-sized body shape. It's often seen as the "all-rounder" of the acoustic world. The Grand Auditorium shape was pioneered by Taylor Guitars.
-   **Sound:** Very balanced across the tonal spectrum (lows, mids, and highs). It has enough volume for strumming but is responsive enough for fingerstyle.
-   **Best for:** Players who want one guitar that can do everything well.

### Dreadnought

-   **Description:** This is arguably the most common and iconic acoustic guitar shape, originally designed by Martin Guitars. It has a large, wide body with a "square-shouldered" look.
-   **Sound:** Loud, powerful, and boomy, with a very strong bass response. It's the classic sound of bluegrass and folk music.
-   **Best for:** Strumming, flatpicking, and players who need a lot of volume to compete with other instruments or singers.

### Jumbo / Super Jumbo

-   **Description:** The largest of the standard acoustic body shapes. It has a very large lower bout and a "pinched" waist, giving it a curvy, hourglass figure.
-   **Sound:** Extremely loud and powerful, with a deep, booming bass and a clear high end. It can sometimes have a slightly "scooped" midrange compared to a dreadnought.
-   **Best for:** Hard-strumming rhythm players who need the maximum possible volume and presence. The classic "cowboy chord" guitar. Famous examples include the Gibson J-200.
