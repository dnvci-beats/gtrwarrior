# Humbucker vs. Single-Coil Pickups

## Description

The two most common types of magnetic pickups found in electric guitars are the single-coil and the humbucker. They have different constructions, which results in very different sounds and characteristics. The choice between them is one of the most fundamental decisions in shaping a guitar's tone.

## Content

### Single-Coil Pickups

-   **Construction:** A single-coil pickup consists of one coil of wire wrapped around a set of magnetic pole pieces. This is the original design for electric guitar pickups.
-   **Sound:** Known for their bright, clear, and articulate sound. They have a "sparkle" and "twang" that is highly sought after for genres like country, surf, funk, and pop. They are very sensitive to playing dynamics.
-   **The Hum:** The major drawback of single-coil pickups is that they are susceptible to electromagnetic interference, which results in a characteristic 60-cycle "hum". This can be a problem in environments with a lot of electrical noise or when using high gain.
-   **Famous Guitars:** Fender Stratocaster, Fender Telecaster.
-   **Famous Players:** Jimi Hendrix, Eric Clapton, Stevie Ray Vaughan, Bruce Springsteen.

### Humbucker Pickups

-   **Construction:** A humbucker pickup, invented by Seth Lover at Gibson in 1955, consists of two coils of wire that are reverse-wound with opposite magnetic polarity. This design is clever because it "bucks the hum" – it cancels out the 60-cycle hum that plagues single-coils.
-   **Sound:** Known for their thicker, warmer, and louder sound. They have more midrange and output than single-coils. Because they cancel some of the higher frequencies, they sound less bright and have more sustain. They are the go-to pickup for rock, hard rock, metal, and jazz.
-   **The Hum:** They are very quiet and resistant to noise, which makes them ideal for high-gain playing.
-   **Famous Guitars:** Gibson Les Paul, Gibson SG, Gibson ES-335.
-   **Famous Players:** Slash, Jimmy Page, B.B. King, Angus Young.

### Other Pickup Types

-   **P-90:** A type of single-coil pickup made by Gibson. It's sonically somewhere between a traditional single-coil and a humbucker. It has the bite and clarity of a single-coil but with more midrange growl and output. It still hums.
-   **Coil-Splitting:** Some humbuckers have a feature called "coil-splitting" or "coil-tapping" that allows you to electronically turn off one of the coils, effectively turning the humbucker into a single-coil pickup. This offers the best of both worlds in one guitar.
