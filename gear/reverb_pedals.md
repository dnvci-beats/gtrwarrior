# Reverb Pedals Explained

## Description

Reverb, short for reverberation, is an effect that simulates the sound of a space or room. It's composed of a series of complex, closely spaced echoes that are created when a sound bounces off surfaces. Reverb is used to give a guitar sound a sense of space, depth, and ambiance, preventing it from sounding dry and unnatural.

## Content

### Main Controls on a Reverb Pedal

Reverb pedals can be simple or very complex, but most have these basic controls:

1.  **Decay (or Time/Reverb):** This controls how long the reverb tail lasts before it fades to silence. A short decay time can simulate a small room, while a long decay time can simulate a large cathedral or cavern.
2.  **Level (or Mix/Blend):** This controls how much of the reverb effect is blended with your dry (unaffected) guitar signal.
3.  **Tone (or EQ):** Many reverb pedals have a tone knob that allows you to make the reverb sound brighter or darker.

### Types of Reverb

Different types of reverb simulate different kinds of physical spaces or use different technologies.

1.  **Spring Reverb:**
    -   The classic guitar reverb sound, originally found in Fender amplifiers in the 1960s.
    -   It works by sending the signal through a set of physical springs in a tank.
    -   Known for its "drippy," "boingy," and splashy sound. It's the signature sound of surf rock.
    -   **Famous Examples:** Fender '63 Spring Reverb unit, many pedals that emulate this sound.

2.  **Plate Reverb:**
    -   An early studio reverb that worked by vibrating a large metal plate.
    -   Known for its bright, dense, and smooth sound. It was a staple of studio recordings in the 60s and 70s.

3.  **Hall/Room Reverb:**
    -   These are digital algorithms designed to simulate the sound of a real acoustic space, like a concert hall, a chamber, or a small room. They are often the most "natural" sounding reverbs.

4.  **Shimmer Reverb:**
    -   A modern, digital reverb effect that adds a layer of pitch-shifted harmony (usually an octave up) to the reverb tail.
    -   This creates a lush, ethereal, "angelic" sound that is popular in ambient and worship music.
    -   **Famous Examples:** Strymon BlueSky and BigSky.

### Where to Place Reverb in Your Signal Chain

Reverb is almost always placed at the **end** of your pedalboard signal chain. You want to add space to your *entire* sound, including any overdrive, modulation, or delay effects that come before it. Placing reverb before distortion, for example, can result in a messy, washed-out sound.
