# Delay Pedals Explained

## Description

A delay pedal is a type of audio effect that records an input signal and then plays it back after a period of time. It creates echoes or repeats of the notes you play. Delay is a fundamental effect used in almost every genre of music to add space, depth, and rhythmic complexity to a guitar part.

## Content

### Main Controls on a Delay Pedal

Most delay pedals have three basic controls:

1.  **Time (or Delay):** This controls the time between the original note and the first repeat. This is usually measured in milliseconds (ms). A short delay time creates a "slapback" echo effect, common in rockabilly. A long delay time creates distinct, spaced-out echoes.
2.  **Feedback (or Repeats/Regen):** This controls how many times the signal is repeated. At a low setting, you might only get one or two repeats. At a high setting, the repeats will continue for a long time, and can even build into self-oscillation, creating a wall of sound.
3.  **Level (or Mix/Blend):** This controls the volume of the repeats. At a low setting, the echoes are subtle and in the background. At a high setting, the echoes can be as loud as the original signal.

### Types of Delay

The character of a delay pedal is often defined by the technology it uses to create the repeats.

1.  **Analog Delay:**
    -   Uses "bucket-brigade" chips to store and forward the signal.
    -   Known for its warm, dark, and somewhat "lo-fi" sound. Each repeat becomes a little more degraded and distorted, which many players find very musical.
    -   Typically has shorter delay times than digital delays.
    -   **Famous Examples:** MXR Carbon Copy, Boss DM-2.

2.  **Digital Delay:**
    -   Uses digital signal processing (DSP) to create the repeats.
    -   Known for its clean, clear, and precise repeats. The echoes are perfect copies of the original signal.
    -   Can offer very long delay times and advanced features like tap tempo.
    -   **Famous Examples:** Boss DD series (DD-3, DD-7, DD-8), Strymon TimeLine.

3.  **Tape Echo:**
    -   The original form of delay, which used a physical loop of magnetic tape to record and play back the signal.
    -   Known for its warm, saturated, and slightly unpredictable sound. The repeats have a characteristic "wow and flutter" (slight pitch modulation) that is very desirable.
    -   Original tape echo machines are large and unreliable, but many modern digital pedals are designed to emulate their sound.
    -   **Famous Examples:** Maestro Echoplex, Roland Space Echo. Many pedals, like the Strymon El Capistan, emulate these units.

### Common Uses

-   **Slapback Echo:** A single, quick repeat to fatten up a guitar tone.
-   **Rhythmic Delay:** Setting the delay time to a specific rhythmic subdivision of the song's tempo (e.g., a dotted-eighth note delay, famously used by U2's The Edge).
-   **Ambient Textures:** Using long feedback and high mix settings to create a "wash" of sound for atmospheric pads.
