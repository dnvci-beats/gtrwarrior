# Slide Guitar Basics

## Description

Slide guitar is a technique where the player uses an object, typically a glass or metal tube called a "slide," to press down on the strings instead of their fingers. The slide is moved along the strings to create smooth, gliding transitions in pitch (glissando) and a vocal-like vibrato. It's a cornerstone of blues, country, and Hawaiian music.

## Content

### The Gear

-   **The Slide:** Slides come in various materials (glass, metal, ceramic), weights, and sizes. Glass slides (like a wine bottleneck, the original slide) have a warmer, smoother tone. Metal slides are brighter and have more sustain. The slide should fit comfortably on one of your fretting hand fingers, usually the ring or pinky finger.
-   **The Guitar:** While you can play slide on any guitar, it's often easier on a guitar with a slightly higher **action** (the distance between the strings and the fretboard). This prevents the slide from rattling against the frets.
-   **Open Tunings:** Slide is often played in an open tuning, where the open strings are tuned to a major chord (e.g., Open G, Open D, Open E). This allows you to play a full chord by laying the slide straight across a single fret.

### The Technique

1.  **Fretting with the Slide:**
    -   Place the slide on your chosen finger.
    -   Lightly touch the slide to the strings. Do not press down hard like you would with your fingers. The goal is to float over the frets.
    -   **Crucially, you must place the slide directly *over* the metal fret wire, not behind it as you would with your fingertips.** This is the key to playing in tune.

2.  **Muting:** Muting is absolutely essential for clean slide playing.
    -   **Behind the Slide:** Use the fingers of your fretting hand that are behind the slide (e.g., your index and middle fingers if the slide is on your ring finger) to lightly rest on the strings and mute unwanted noise and overtones.
    -   **Picking Hand Muting:** You can also use your picking hand's palm to mute strings you aren't playing.

3.  **Vibrato:** Slide vibrato is created by moving the slide back and forth horizontally over the target fret. This creates the characteristic "singing" quality of slide guitar.

### Common Open Tunings

-   **Open G:** D-G-D-G-B-D (from low to high). Famously used by Keith Richards.
-   **Open D:** D-A-D-F#-A-D. Famously used by Elmore James.
-   **Open E:** E-B-E-G#-B-E. A favorite of Duane Allman. (Note: Open E is the same intervals as Open D, just a whole step higher).

## Resources

-   Slide guitar is all about ear training, as you don't have frets to guide your intonation. Practice sliding into notes from below or above and develop a controlled vibrato. Listen to slide masters like Duane Allman, Derek Trucks, Ry Cooder, and Sonny Landreth.
