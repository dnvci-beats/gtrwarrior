# String Bending

## Description

String bending is an iconic guitar technique that involves pushing or pulling a string across the fretboard to raise its pitch. It's one of the most expressive techniques available to a guitarist, allowing them to emulate the human voice and add a huge amount of emotion and character to their playing. It is a cornerstone of blues, rock, and country guitar.

## Content

### How to Bend a String

1.  **Fret the note:** Fret a note as you normally would, for example, the 7th fret of the G string.
2.  **Use supporting fingers:** This is the most important part of good bending technique. Use the fingers behind your fretting finger to support the bend. If you are fretting with your ring finger, your middle and index fingers should be on the same string, right behind it, ready to help push.
3.  **Push the string:** Push the string towards the ceiling (or pull it towards the floor, which is less common but also works). The motion should come from rotating your wrist and forearm, not from your fingers alone. This provides strength and control.
4.  **Control the pitch:** The goal is to bend the note up to a specific pitch, usually a half step (one fret higher) or a whole step (two frets higher). This requires practice and ear training. You can check your pitch by playing the target note (e.g., the 8th fret for a half-step bend, or the 9th fret for a whole-step bend) and then trying to match it with your bend.

### Types of Bends

-   **Bend:** The standard bend described above.
-   **Bend and Release:** Bend the note up to pitch and then release it back to the original note.
-   **Pre-bend:** Bend the string to the target pitch *before* you pick it. Then, pick the string and release the bend.
-   **Unison Bend:** Bend a note on one string until it matches the pitch of a note played on an adjacent string. This creates a powerful, chorus-like effect.
