# Tapping

## Description

Tapping is a guitar technique where the player uses their fingertips to "tap" on the fretboard to sound notes, similar to how a piano is played. It allows for the performance of fast, wide-interval lines that would be difficult or impossible to play with conventional picking and fretting. The technique was popularized in the late 1970s and 1980s by players like Eddie Van Halen.

## Content

### The Basic Technique

1.  **The Tap:** Use the index or middle finger of your picking hand to firmly tap a fret on a string. The force of the tap should be enough to sound the note clearly without needing to pick the string.
2.  **The Pull-off:** To play a lower note after the tapped note, you "pull off" the tapping finger with a slight flicking or plucking motion. This causes a lower note, held by your fretting hand, to sound.
3.  **Hammer-ons and Pull-offs (Fretting Hand):** Tapping is almost always combined with conventional hammer-ons and pull-offs in the fretting hand.

A typical tapping lick involves a three-note pattern:
1.  A low note fretted normally.
2.  A higher note played with a hammer-on.
3.  A much higher note played with a tap from the picking hand.
4.  The sequence is then reversed using pull-offs.

### Example Lick (A minor)

Here is a classic tapping pattern in A minor, similar to what you might hear in Eddie Van Halen's "Eruption".

-   **Fretting Hand:** Your index finger is on the 5th fret of the high E string.
-   **Tapping Hand:** Your index finger will tap the 12th fret.

The sequence:
1.  Fret the 5th fret (A).
2.  Tap the 12th fret (E) and let it ring.
3.  Pull-off from the 12th fret back to the 5th fret.
4.  Hammer-on to the 8th fret (C) with your fretting hand's pinky.
5.  Pull-off from the 8th fret back to the 5th fret.
6.  Repeat the pattern.

```
     T         p         h     p
e|--12--------5---------8-----5-------|
B|------------------------------------|
G|------------------------------------|
D|------------------------------------|
A|------------------------------------|
E|------------------------------------|
```
(T = Tap, p = pull-off, h = hammer-on)

### Variations

-   **Two-Handed Tapping:** More advanced players use multiple fingers on both hands to tap complex chordal and contrapuntal patterns. Players like Stanley Jordan and Jennifer Batten are famous for this style.
-   **String Skipping:** Tapping is not limited to one string. You can create arpeggios and other patterns by tapping across multiple strings.

## Resources

-   This technique requires practice to develop the strength and coordination for clean notes. Start slowly and focus on getting a clear sound from each tap and pull-off. Many video lessons online can provide visual guidance.
