# Hammer-ons and Pull-offs

## Description

Hammer-ons and pull-offs are two essential legato techniques on the guitar. Legato means "tied together" in Italian, and these techniques allow you to play notes smoothly without picking every single one. They are fundamental for playing fluid lines and are used in virtually every style of music.

## Content

### Hammer-ons

A hammer-on is when you play a note by "hammering" a finger of your fretting hand down onto the fretboard, causing the string to sound a higher note without picking it again.

**How to perform a hammer-on:**
1.  Pick a note on a string (e.g., the 5th fret of the G string).
2.  While that note is still ringing, use another finger (e.g., your ring finger) to firmly and quickly press down on a higher fret on the same string (e.g., the 7th fret).
3.  The impact of your finger should be strong enough to make the new note sound out clearly.

### Pull-offs

A pull-off is the opposite of a hammer-on. It's when you play a note by "pulling" a finger off a fret, causing a lower note that is already fretted to sound.

**How to perform a pull-off:**
1.  Place two fingers on the same string at different frets (e.g., your ring finger on the 7th fret and your index finger on the 5th fret of the G string).
2.  Pick the higher note (the one on the 7th fret).
3.  While the note is ringing, pull your ring finger off the string with a slight downward plucking motion. This will cause the lower note (held by your index finger) to sound.
4.  The "plucking" motion is key to getting a clear sound. Don't just lift your finger straight off the string.

### Combining Hammer-ons and Pull-offs

These two techniques are often used together to create fast, flowing musical phrases called "trills" or "slurs". For example, you could play a repeating pattern of 5th fret to 7th fret and back on the G string by picking the first note and then using a hammer-on and a pull-off for the rest.
