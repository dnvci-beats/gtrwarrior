# Vibrato

## Description

Vibrato is one of the most expressive techniques on the guitar. It involves subtly and rapidly modulating the pitch of a sustained note, making it "wobble" up and down. Good vibrato can make a guitar sound like it's singing and is a key component of a guitarist's individual "voice" and style.

## Content

### How it Works

Vibrato is essentially a series of very small, rapid string bends and releases. The goal is to move the pitch of the note slightly sharp, back to the original pitch, and sometimes slightly flat, in a smooth and controlled rhythm.

### Types of Vibrato

There are several ways to achieve vibrato on a guitar:

1.  **Wrist-Based Vibrato (Most Common for Electric Guitar):**
    -   This is the style used by most blues and rock players like B.B. King and Eric Clapton.
    -   Fret a note as you normally would.
    -   The motion comes from rotating your wrist, similar to turning a doorknob. Your finger stays relatively locked, and the wrist rotation causes the string to be bent and released in a small, controlled way.
    -   The motion is parallel to the neck of the guitar.

2.  **Classical Vibrato:**
    -   This style is used in classical guitar and by some rock players like Yngwie Malmsteen.
    -   The motion comes from the finger itself, moving back and forth along the direction of the string.
    -   This produces a much more subtle, shimmering vibrato.

3.  **Whammy Bar Vibrato:**
    -   Using the guitar's vibrato system (whammy bar), you can create a very smooth and even pitch modulation. This is common in surf music and by players like Jeff Beck.

### Developing Good Vibrato

-   **Rhythm:** Good vibrato is in time with the music. Practice your vibrato with a metronome, aiming for an even rhythm (e.g., eighth-note or sixteenth-note pulses).
-   **Control:** You should be able to control the **width** (how far you bend the pitch) and the **speed** (how fast you modulate it) of your vibrato. A slow, wide vibrato can sound relaxed and soulful, while a fast, narrow vibrato can sound tense and aggressive.
-   **Intonation:** Be careful that the center of your vibrato is the correct, in-tune note. You are generally bending the note sharp and releasing it back to the original pitch, not going flat.

### Why is it so important?

Vibrato is what gives life to sustained notes. Without it, notes can sound static and uninteresting. A player's vibrato is often as recognizable as their signature.

## Resources

-   Listen closely to your favorite guitarists and pay attention to their vibrato. Try to emulate their speed, width, and style. This is a technique that is more about "feel" than precise science.
