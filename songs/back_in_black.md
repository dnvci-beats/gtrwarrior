# Back in Black - AC/DC

## Description

"Back in Black" is one of AC/DC's most famous songs and a true hard rock anthem. Released in 1980, it features the signature, bone-crunching guitar work of brothers Angus and Malcolm Young. The main riff is a masterclass in powerful, open-position rock guitar.

## Content

### The Sound

The iconic AC/DC sound comes from a Gibson SG (Angus) and a Gretsch Jet Firebird (Malcolm) plugged into Marshall tube amps that are turned up loud. The sound is overdriven but not overly saturated or distorted. It's a powerful, clear crunch.

### The Main Riff

The main riff is built from three chords: E, D, and A. The genius is in the rhythm and the space between the chords. It is one of the most recognizable riffs in rock.

The sequence is as follows:

**Chord 1: E major** (played as an open position power chord)
```
e|-----------------|
B|-----------------|
G|-1---------------|
D|-2---------------|
A|-2---------------|
E|-0---------------|
```

**Chord 2: D major** (played as an open position power chord)
```
e|-----------------|
B|-3---------------|
G|-2---------------|
D|-0---------------|
A|-----------------|
E|-----------------|
```

**Chord 3: A major** (played as an open position power chord)
```
e|-----------------|
B|-2---------------|
G|-2---------------|
D|-2---------------|
A|-0---------------|
E|-----------------|
```

The rhythm is roughly: **E** (let ring), **E** (staccato), **E** (staccato)... **D** (let ring)... **A** (let ring). This is followed by a classic bluesy lick before the riff repeats.

### The Verse Riff

The riff behind the verses is a simple but powerful progression of power chords:
**E5 - G5 - A5**
