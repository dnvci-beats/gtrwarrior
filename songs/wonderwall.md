# Wonderwall - Strumming Pattern

## Description

"Wonderwall" by Oasis is one of the most famous acoustic guitar songs of the 90s. Its distinctive, syncopated strumming pattern is key to its sound. The song is originally played with a capo on the 2nd fret. The chords relative to the capo are Em7, G, Dsus4, and A7sus4.

## Content

### The Chords (relative to capo on 2nd fret)

The chord shapes are what make this song unique. They are not standard open chords.

-   **Em7:** 022033
-   **G:** 320033
-   **Dsus4:** xx0233
-   **A7sus4:** x02033

Notice that the high G and D strings (3rd fret on the B and high E strings) are held down for all the chords. This creates a drone that is characteristic of the song.

### The Strumming Pattern

The strumming pattern is syncopated, meaning some of the strums happen on the "and" of the beat (the off-beat). A common way to play it is:

**|1   &   2   &   3   &   4   & |**
**|D   D U   U D   D   D U   U D |**

Let's break that down:
-   **Beat 1:** Downstroke on the 1, another downstroke on the 'and' of 1.
-   **Beat 2:** Upstroke on the 'and' of 2.
-   **Beat 3:** Downstroke on the 3.
-   **Beat 4:** Downstroke on the 4, followed by an upstroke on the 'and' of 4.

This pattern can be tricky at first. It's often simplified for beginners. A more basic pattern that still captures the feel is:

**|1   &   2   &   3   &   4   & |**
**|D       D U     U D     U D U |**

### Chord Progression

The main progression for the verse and chorus is:
**Em7 | G | Dsus4 | A7sus4 |** (two beats per chord)

This means you will play half of the strumming pattern on Em7, and the other half on G, and so on.
