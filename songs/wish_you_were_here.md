# Wish You Were Here - Pink Floyd

## Description

"Wish You Were Here" is the title track from Pink Floyd's 1975 album of the same name. The song's iconic opening was recorded to sound like it was coming from a tinny, old-timey radio, which then transitions into a full, rich acoustic guitar sound. The intro riff is a classic piece of acoustic guitar work.

## Content

### The Sound

The main riff is played on a 12-string acoustic guitar, which gives it a full, shimmering sound. The second guitar part, which plays the melodic solo, is played on a 6-string acoustic. The tone is natural and unadorned.

### The Riff's Structure

The song is in the key of G Major. The intro riff is based around G and C/G chord shapes, with melodic hammer-ons and open strings that create a beautiful, memorable melody.

The riff is composed of two main parts that alternate:
1.  **The G Lick:** This part is based on a G major chord. The melody is created by using hammer-ons on the A and D strings (from open to the 2nd fret) while strumming the G chord.
2.  **The C/G Lick:** The fretting hand then moves to a C/G chord shape (like a C chord, but with the G note in the bass on the low E string). A similar melodic idea with hammer-ons is played over this shape.

The progression generally alternates between these two shapes, creating a feeling of G - C - G. The intro solo is played over this repeating pattern.

A very simplified representation of the main melodic idea on the G chord:
-   Strum a G chord, but leave the A and D strings open.
-   Hammer-on to the 2nd fret of the A string.
-   Strum again.
-   Hammer-on to the 2nd fret of the D string.

### The Main Song Chords

The verse and chorus of the song use a simple, classic chord progression:
**C/G - D/F# - Am - G - D/F# - C/G**
