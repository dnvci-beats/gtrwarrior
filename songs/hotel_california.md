# Hotel California - The Eagles

## Description

"Hotel California" by The Eagles is one of the most iconic songs in rock history, famous for its haunting lyrics and legendary dual guitar solos. The verse features a beautiful and memorable chord progression played on a 12-string acoustic guitar, with a capo on the 7th fret.

## Content

### The Chord Progression

The song is in the key of B minor. However, by placing a capo on the 7th fret, the chords are played using familiar open-position shapes, as if the song were in E minor. All chord names here are relative to the capo.

The main progression for the verses is:

**Am - E7 - G - D - F - C - Dm - E7**

This progression is a masterclass in using chords both inside and outside the key to create a sense of unease and drama.

Let's break it down (relative to the capo, in the key of Am):

-   **Am:** The i chord. Establishes the minor key.
-   **E7:** The V7 chord. This is the dominant of Am and resolves strongly back to it.
-   **G:** The bVII chord. This is a borrowed chord from the parallel major key (A Major). It adds a bit of brightness.
-   **D:** The IV chord. A standard chord in A minor.
-   **F:** The bVI chord. Another standard chord in a minor key.
-   **C:** The bIII chord. Another borrowed chord.
-   **Dm:** The iv chord.
-   **E7:** The V7 chord again, leading back to the top of the progression.

The strumming is generally a relaxed, arpeggiated pattern. It's not a hard strum, but rather a gentle pick or fingerstyle pattern that lets the notes of the 12-string guitar ring out. A simple approach would be to use downstrokes on the beat and add some upstroke flourishes.

### The Sound

The combination of the high-strung 12-string guitar with the capo high on the neck gives the song its signature shimmering, ethereal quality.
