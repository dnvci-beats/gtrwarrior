# Johnny B. Goode - Chuck Berry

## Description

The opening guitar riff to "Johnny B. Goode" by Chuck Berry is one of the most important and influential pieces of music in the history of rock and roll. Released in 1958, this riff has been quoted, copied, and paid homage to by countless guitarists. It's a perfect example of Chuck Berry's blend of blues and country that helped to define the sound of rock guitar.

## Content

### The Sound

Chuck Berry typically played Gibson hollow-body guitars, like the ES-350T. The sound is a clean, slightly overdriven tube amp tone. It's bright, punchy, and clear.

### The Riff

The song is in the key of Bb Major. The riff is based on the Bb blues scale. It's famous for its use of double-stops (playing two notes at once) and string bends.

The tab below is a simplified version of the main ideas in the intro.

**Part 1: The Opening "Call"**
The riff starts with a classic bluesy pickup.
```
e|----------------------|
B|--6--6--8--6----------|
G|--7--7--8--7----------|
D|----------------------|
A|----------------------|
E|----------------------|
```
(This is played with a slide up to the first double-stop).

**Part 2: The Main Phrase**
This part features the iconic double-stop bends.
```
e|----------------------------------------------------|
B|----------------------------------------------------|
G|--6b8-6b8-6b8-6b8-6b8-6b8--8-6-----------------------|
D|-------------------------------8--------------------|
A|----------------------------------------------------|
E|----------------------------------------------------|
```
(The "6b8" means bend the note at the 6th fret of the G string up to the pitch of the 8th fret).

**Part 3: The Descending Run**
The riff ends with a fast descending run based on the blues scale.
```
e|----------------------------------------------------|
B|--9--6----------------------------------------------|
G|--------8--6----------------------------------------|
D|--------------8--6----------------------------------|
A|--------------------8--7--6-------------------------|
E|-----------------------------9--6-------------------|
```

### The Rhythm

The song is based on a standard 12-bar blues progression in Bb. The rhythm guitar part would typically play shuffle or boogie-woogie style chords.
